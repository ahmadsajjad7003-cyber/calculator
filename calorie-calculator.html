What are Calories?

Calories are units of energy. Your body needs calories from food to function — to breathe, pump blood, digest food, and move. When you eat more calories than you burn, your body stores the excess as fat. When you eat fewer calories than you burn, your body uses stored fat for energy, leading to weight loss.

Understanding your daily calorie needs is the first step toward achieving and maintaining a healthy weight.

Calorie Formula (Mifflin-St Jeor)

Men: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5

Women: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161

TDEE: BMR × Activity Multiplier

Weight Loss: TDEE − 500 cal/day (for 0.5 kg/week loss)

Worked Example

A 30-year-old male, 70 kg, 175 cm, moderately active:

  1. BMR = 10(70) + 6.25(175) − 5(30) + 5 = 1,648.75 cal
  2. TDEE = 1,648.75 × 1.55 = 2,555.56 cal/day
  3. To lose 0.5 kg/week: 2,555.56 − 500 = 2,055 cal/day

Activity Level Guide

  • Sedentary: Desk job, little or no exercise
  • Lightly Active: Light exercise 1-3 days/week
  • Moderately Active: Moderate exercise 3-5 days/week
  • Very Active: Hard exercise 6-7 days/week
  • Extra Active: Very hard exercise, physical job, or athletic training

Healthy Weight Loss Tips

  • Safe rate: Lose 0.5–1 kg (1–2 lbs) per week.
  • Protein: Eat plenty of protein to preserve muscle.
  • Water: Drink 8+ glasses of water daily.
  • Sleep: 7–9 hours of sleep supports weight loss.
  • Exercise: Combine cardio with strength training.
  • Tracking: Log your food to stay on target.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat per day?

The average adult needs 1,800–2,500 calories per day, depending on age, gender, weight, height, and activity level.

What is BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at rest to maintain basic functions.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

To lose about 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week, create a deficit of about 500 calories per day from your TDEE.

What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total calories you burn per day, including BMR plus physical activity.
