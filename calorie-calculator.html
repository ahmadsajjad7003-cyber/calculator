What is a Calorie?

A calorie is a unit of energy. In nutrition, when we say "calorie," we usually mean a kilocalorie (kcal) — the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kilogram of water by 1°C. Your body uses calories from food for everything: breathing, moving, thinking, and even sleeping.

Eat more calories than you burn, and you gain weight. Eat fewer, and you lose weight. Eat the same amount, and you maintain weight.

How Daily Calorie Needs Are Calculated

Your daily calorie needs depend on two things:

  1. BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate): Calories burned at complete rest.
  2. Activity Level: Additional calories burned through daily movement and exercise.

The combined number is your TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) — the calories you need to maintain your current weight.

Calorie Formulas

Step 1 — BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor):

Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5

Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

Step 2 — TDEE:

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Step 3 — Goal Calories:

Weight Loss = TDEE − 250 to 1000 cal

Weight Gain = TDEE + 250 to 500 cal

Activity Multipliers

Activity LevelMultiplier
Sedentary (little/no exercise)1.2
Light (1-3 days/week)1.375
Moderate (3-5 days/week)1.55
Heavy (6-7 days/week)1.725
Athlete (2x/day)1.9

Worked Example

Example: 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm, moderate activity.

BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 1,649 cal

TDEE = 1,649 × 1.55 = 2,556 cal/day (maintenance)

For 0.5 kg/week weight loss: 2,556 − 500 = 2,056 cal/day

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

To lose about 0.5 kg per week, eat 500 calories less than your TDEE. For slower weight loss, use a 250-calorie deficit. Never go below 1,200 calories for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision.

What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total number of calories you burn in a day, including BMR plus all activity. It's the number of calories you need to maintain your current weight.

How accurate is a calorie calculator?

Calorie calculators give estimates within 10-15% of actual needs. Use the result as a starting point and adjust based on real-world results over 2-3 weeks.

Do I need to count calories forever?

No. Many people count calories temporarily to learn portion sizes and their body's needs, then transition to intuitive eating.

Are all calories the same?

For weight, mostly yes — 500 calories of chicken and 500 calories of cake affect weight similarly. But they affect health, hunger, and muscle very differently. Protein, fiber, and whole foods are more satisfying and better for body composition.

Why am I not losing weight on a deficit?

Common causes: underestimating portions, not tracking accurately, water retention, muscle gain masking fat loss, or a slower metabolism than expected. Track food precisely for 2 weeks to see the truth.

What is a safe rate of weight loss?

0.5 to 1 kg per week is generally safe and sustainable. Faster loss often leads to muscle loss, nutrient deficiency, and rebound weight gain.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

If you set your activity level to include exercise, no. If you set it to sedentary and exercise heavily, you may need to eat some back to avoid excessive deficit.

How much protein do I need?

For weight loss: 1.6-2.2 g per kg of body weight to preserve muscle. For maintenance: 1.2-1.6 g/kg. Protein is the most satiating and muscle-sparing macronutrient.
