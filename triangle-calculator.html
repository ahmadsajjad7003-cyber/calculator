What is a Triangle?

A triangle is a three-sided polygon with three vertices. It's the simplest polygon and one of the most fundamental shapes in geometry. Triangles are used everywhere in architecture, engineering, navigation, and art because of their structural stability.

The three angles of any triangle always add up to 180° (or π radians).

Triangle Formulas

Perimeter: P = A + B + C

Area (Heron's Formula): s = (A+B+C)/2, Area = √(s(s−A)(s−B)(s−C))

Angle A (Law of Cosines): cos(A) = (b² + c² − a²) / (2bc)

Worked Example

Triangle with sides 3, 4, 5:

  1. Perimeter = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12
  2. s = 12/2 = 6
  3. Area = √(6 × 3 × 2 × 1) = √36 = 6
  4. Angles: A ≈ 36.87°, B ≈ 53.13°, C = 90° (Right triangle!)

Types of Triangles

By Sides:

  • Equilateral: All three sides equal (all angles 60°).
  • Isosceles: Two sides equal (two angles equal).
  • Scalene: All three sides different.

By Angles:

  • Right: One angle = 90° (a² + b² = c²).
  • Acute: All angles less than 90°.
  • Obtuse: One angle greater than 90°.

Triangle Inequality Theorem

For any triangle, the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side:

  • A + B > C
  • A + C > B
  • B + C > A

If any of these fail, a triangle cannot exist with those side lengths.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is Heron's formula?

Heron's formula calculates the area from three sides: Area = √(s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)), where s is the semi-perimeter.

What is the Triangle Inequality Theorem?

The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side. If not, the triangle is impossible.

What are the types of triangles?

Equilateral, Isosceles, Scalene (by sides); Right, Acute, Obtuse (by angles).

How do I find the angles of a triangle?

Use the Law of Cosines: cos(A) = (b² + c² − a²) / (2bc). The angles always add up to 180°.
