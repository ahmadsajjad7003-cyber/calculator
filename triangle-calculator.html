What is a Triangle?

A triangle is a three-sided polygon. The sum of its interior angles is always 180°. Triangles are classified by their sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) or angles (acute, right, obtuse).

Triangle Formulas

Perimeter: P = a + b + c

Heron's Formula (Area): A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]

Where s = semi-perimeter = (a + b + c) / 2

Law of Cosines (Angles): cos(A) = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc

Triangle Inequality Theorem

For any triangle, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must be greater than the length of the third side. If this rule is violated, the triangle cannot exist.

Triangle Types

  • Equilateral: All three sides equal
  • Isosceles: Two sides equal
  • Scalene: All sides different
  • Right: One angle is 90° (a² + b² = c²)
  • Acute: All angles less than 90°
  • Obtuse: One angle greater than 90°

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Heron's formula?

Heron's formula calculates the area of a triangle using only the three side lengths — no height needed.

How do I know if three sides form a triangle?

Check the triangle inequality: a + b > c, b + c > a, and a + c > b. If all three are true, a valid triangle exists.

What is the sum of angles in a triangle?

Always 180° in Euclidean geometry — regardless of the triangle's shape or size.

What is a right triangle?

A triangle with one 90° angle. It satisfies the Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.
