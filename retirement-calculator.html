What is Retirement Planning?

Retirement planning is the process of determining your retirement income goals and the actions needed to achieve them. It involves identifying sources of income, estimating expenses, and implementing a savings program to build a retirement corpus that will support you after you stop working.

With rising life expectancy, people now spend 20-30 years in retirement. Proper planning is essential to maintain your lifestyle without financial stress.

Retirement Formula

Future Value of Current Savings: FV = PV × (1+r)^n

Future Value of Monthly Savings: FV = PMT × [(1+r)^n − 1] / r

Total Corpus: FV of current savings + FV of monthly savings

Where: PV = Present savings, PMT = Monthly savings, r = Monthly rate, n = Total months

Worked Example

Age 30, retiring at 65, $50,000 saved, $1,000/month savings, 7% return:

  1. Years to retirement: 65 − 30 = 35 years
  2. Monthly rate: 7 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.005833
  3. Total months: 35 × 12 = 420
  4. Future value of $50,000: $557,000+
  5. Future value of $1,000/month: $1,560,000+
  6. Total corpus: ~$2.1 million

Key Retirement Planning Rules

  • Save 15% of income: A common rule for retirement savings.
  • Rule of 25: Multiply desired annual retirement income by 25 to estimate needed corpus. Example: $50,000/year × 25 = $1,250,000 corpus.
  • 4% Rule: Withdraw 4% of your corpus annually — historically sustainable for 30 years.
  • Start early: Compound interest rewards those who start saving in their 20s.
  • Diversify: Mix stocks, bonds, and other investments.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much should I save for retirement?

Most experts recommend saving 15% of your income for retirement. The exact amount depends on your current age, retirement age, expenses, and lifestyle.

What is a retirement corpus?

A retirement corpus is the total amount you accumulate by retirement — including current savings, future monthly savings, and investment growth.

At what age should I start saving for retirement?

The earlier, the better. Starting in your 20s gives compound interest more time to work. But it's never too late — even starting at 40 helps.

How long will my retirement corpus last?

It depends on your corpus, monthly expenses, and life expectancy. Our calculator estimates monthly income based on these factors.
