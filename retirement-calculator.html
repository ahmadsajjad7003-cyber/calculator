What is Retirement Planning?

Retirement planning is the process of determining your retirement income goals and the actions needed to achieve them. It involves estimating how much you'll need to live comfortably after you stop working, then saving and investing consistently to reach that target.

The earlier you start, the less you need to save each month — because compound interest does the heavy lifting over decades.

Retirement Formula

Future Value of Current Savings:

FV = PV × (1+r)^n

Future Value of Monthly Savings:

FV = PMT × [(1+r)^n − 1] / r

Where:

  • FV = future value
  • PV = present value (current savings)
  • PMT = monthly savings
  • r = monthly return rate
  • n = number of months

Worked Example

Example: Age 30, retire at 65, $50,000 saved, $1,000/month, 7% annual return.

Years to retirement: 35 → Months: 420

Monthly rate: 7% ÷ 12 = 0.5833%

FV of current savings = $50,000 × (1.005833)^420 ≈ $572,000

FV of monthly savings = $1,000 × [(1.005833)^420 − 1] / 0.005833 ≈ $1,843,000

Total corpus ≈ $2,415,000

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I save for retirement?

Most experts recommend saving 15% of your income for retirement, starting in your 20s. The exact amount depends on your desired lifestyle and retirement age.

What is a retirement corpus?

A retirement corpus is the total amount you accumulate by retirement — including current savings, future monthly savings, and investment growth.

What is the 4% rule?

The 4% rule suggests withdrawing 4% of your retirement corpus annually. So a $1 million corpus would give $40,000 per year, adjusted for inflation.

At what age should I start saving for retirement?

The earlier, the better. Starting in your 20s gives compound interest more time to work. But even starting in your 40s is better than not starting at all.

How long will my retirement corpus last?

It depends on your corpus, monthly expenses, life expectancy, and post-retirement returns. The 4% rule is a useful starting guide.

What return rate should I assume?

Historically, the S&P 500 has returned about 7% per year after inflation. For conservative planning, many use 5-7%. For retirement accounts, a diversified portfolio often assumes 6-7%.

Should I include Social Security?

Yes, if you expect to receive it. Social Security replaces about 40% of pre-retirement income for the average worker. Subtract expected Social Security from your needed income.

What about inflation?

Inflation reduces purchasing power over time. Use a "real" return (return minus inflation) in your calculations, or explicitly adjust your target income for inflation.

What is the difference between a 401(k) and an IRA?

A 401(k) is offered by employers, often with matching contributions. An IRA is opened individually. Both offer tax advantages for retirement savings.
