What is BMR?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It is the number of calories your body needs to perform basic life-sustaining functions while at complete rest — breathing, circulating blood, regulating body temperature, growing cells, and repairing tissues.

Think of BMR as the "idle speed" of your body's engine. Even if you stayed in bed all day, your body would still burn this many calories just to stay alive.

Why BMR Matters

BMR typically accounts for 60-75% of your total daily calorie burn. Understanding your BMR helps you:

  • Plan a weight loss or weight gain diet
  • Determine your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)
  • Understand how many calories you need to maintain weight
  • Track fitness progress more accurately

BMR Formulas

Mifflin-St Jeor (most accurate for general population):

Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5

Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

Harris-Benedict (Revised):

Men: BMR = 13.397 × weight + 4.799 × height − 5.677 × age + 88.362

Women: BMR = 9.247 × weight + 3.098 × height − 4.330 × age + 447.593

Worked Example

Example: A 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm tall, using Mifflin-St Jeor.

BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 30 + 5

BMR = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5

BMR ≈ 1,649 calories/day

This means he burns about 1,649 calories per day just by existing. Any activity adds more on top.

Factors That Affect BMR

  • Muscle Mass: More muscle means higher BMR — muscle burns more calories at rest than fat.
  • Age: BMR typically decreases with age, partly due to muscle loss.
  • Gender: Men generally have higher BMR due to greater muscle mass.
  • Body Size: Larger bodies require more calories at rest.
  • Genetics: Some people naturally have faster or slower metabolisms.
  • Hormones: Thyroid issues can significantly affect BMR.
  • Temperature: Extreme cold or heat can temporarily increase BMR.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain vital functions like breathing, circulation, and cell production.

What is a normal BMR?

A typical adult BMR ranges from 1,200 to 2,000 calories per day. Men generally have higher BMR than women due to greater muscle mass.

How can I increase my BMR?

You can increase BMR by building muscle through strength training, eating enough protein, getting adequate sleep, and staying physically active.

What is the difference between BMR and RMR?

BMR is measured under strict laboratory conditions (complete rest, fasting). RMR (Resting Metabolic Rate) is measured under less strict conditions and is slightly higher. In practice, they are often used interchangeably.

Is BMR the same as TDEE?

No. BMR is calories burned at complete rest. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is BMR plus all activity — exercise, walking, digestion, etc. TDEE is always higher than BMR.

Can I lose weight eating only my BMR?

Eating below your BMR for long periods is not recommended. It can slow your metabolism and cause muscle loss. A safer approach is to eat 300-500 calories below your TDEE.

Which BMR formula is most accurate?

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is considered the most accurate for the general population. Harris-Benedict is older and slightly overestimates. Katch-McArdle is best if you know your body fat percentage.

Does BMR change with age?

Yes. BMR generally decreases with age, mostly due to loss of muscle mass. Staying active and strength training can slow this decline.

Does eating increase BMR?

Eating temporarily increases metabolism — this is called the Thermic Effect of Food (TEF). Protein has the highest TEF (20-30% of its calories), followed by carbs (5-10%) and fat (0-3%).
