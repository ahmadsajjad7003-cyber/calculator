What is an Average?

An average is a single number that represents the center or typical value of a set of numbers. The most common type of average is the mean, calculated by adding all numbers and dividing by the count. However, there are other measures of central tendency: median, mode, and range.

Averages are used everywhere — in school grades, sports statistics, financial reports, weather data, and scientific research.

Statistical Formulas

Mean: Sum ÷ Count

Median: Middle value of sorted list (average of 2 middle values if even count)

Mode: Most frequently occurring value

Range: Max − Min

Worked Example

For the set: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50

  • Mean: (10+20+30+40+50) ÷ 5 = 150 ÷ 5 = 30
  • Median: Middle value = 30
  • Mode: No mode (all unique)
  • Range: 50 − 10 = 40
  • Sum: 150

For the set: 5, 5, 10, 15, 20

  • Mean: 55 ÷ 5 = 11
  • Median: 10
  • Mode: 5 (appears twice)

Mean vs Median vs Mode

  • Mean: Best for normally distributed data. Affected by outliers.
  • Median: Best when data has outliers or is skewed. Not affected by extreme values.
  • Mode: Best for categorical data (e.g., most common shoe size). Can have multiple modes or none.

Real-World Uses of Averages

  • Education: GPA, class averages, test scores
  • Finance: Average income, stock returns, inflation
  • Sports: Batting average, scoring average, win percentage
  • Weather: Average temperature, rainfall
  • Business: Average sales, customer spend
  • Health: Average heart rate, blood pressure

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between mean, median, and mode?

Mean is sum ÷ count. Median is the middle value. Mode is the most frequent value.

How do I calculate the average?

Add all numbers and divide by how many there are. Average of 10, 20, 30 is 20.

When should I use median instead of mean?

Use median when your data has extreme outliers (e.g., income data with billionaires).

What if there is no mode?

If every number appears once, there's no mode. If two or more numbers tie, there can be multiple modes.
