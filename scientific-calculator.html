What is a Scientific Calculator?

A scientific calculator is an advanced calculator capable of performing calculations beyond basic arithmetic. It includes trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan), logarithms (log, ln), powers and roots, factorials, and constants like π and e. It's essential for students, engineers, scientists, and anyone working with advanced math.

Degrees vs Radians

Angles can be measured in degrees or radians:

  • Degrees: A full circle is 360°. Common in everyday use, navigation, and basic trigonometry.
  • Radians: A full circle is 2π radians (≈ 6.283). Standard in calculus, physics, and advanced math.
  • Conversion: 180° = π radians ≈ 3.14159 radians

Use the Deg/Rad toggle at the top of the calculator to switch between modes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are trigonometric functions in degrees or radians?

This calculator supports both. Use the Deg/Rad toggle to switch. Degrees are more common in everyday use; radians are used in advanced math and physics.

What is the difference between log and ln?

log is the base-10 logarithm. ln is the natural logarithm (base e, where e ≈ 2.71828). Both are used in science, engineering, and math.

What does factorial (!) mean?

n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × ... × 1. For example, 5! = 5×4×3×2×1 = 120. Factorials are used in probability and combinatorics.

How do I calculate a power like 2^10?

Press 2, then xʸ, then 10, then =. The result is 1024. This is the same as 2 raised to the power of 10.

What does the ± button do?

It changes the sign of the current number or expression. For example, pressing 5 then ± gives −5.

Can I use my keyboard?

Yes. Numbers and operators (+, −, ×, ÷, ., parentheses) work directly. Press Enter for =, Backspace to delete, and Escape to clear.

How accurate is this scientific calculator?

It uses JavaScript's IEEE 754 double-precision arithmetic, which is accurate to about 15-17 significant digits. This is the same precision used by most desktop calculators.

What is pi (π)?

Pi is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, approximately 3.14159. It's an irrational number, meaning it has an infinite number of non-repeating decimals.

What is Euler's number (e)?

e is approximately 2.71828. It's the base of natural logarithms and is used in exponential growth, compound interest, and calculus.

Is this scientific calculator free?

Yes, 100% free. All calculations run in your browser — no data is sent to any server.
