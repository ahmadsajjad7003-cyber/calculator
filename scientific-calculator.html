What is a Scientific Calculator?

A scientific calculator is an advanced calculator designed to handle complex mathematical operations beyond basic arithmetic. It includes functions like trigonometry (sin, cos, tan), logarithms (log, ln), powers, roots, factorials, and mathematical constants like π (pi) and e (Euler's number).

Scientific calculators are essential for students, engineers, scientists, and anyone working with advanced math.

How to Use This Scientific Calculator

  1. Choose your angle mode — Degrees or Radians.
  2. Enter your expression — click buttons or use your keyboard.
  3. Press "=" or hit Enter to calculate.
  4. AC clears everything. ⌫ deletes the last character.

Supported Functions

  • Basic: +, −, ×, ÷, decimal point
  • Trigonometry: sin, cos, tan (Degrees/Radians)
  • Logarithms: log (base 10), ln (natural log)
  • Powers & Roots: x^y, √ (square root)
  • Constants: π (3.14159...), e (2.71828...)
  • Factorial: n! (e.g., 5! = 120)
  • Parentheses: ( and ) for grouping

Worked Examples

  • sin(30) in Degrees: = 0.5
  • log(100): = 2
  • ln(e): = 1
  • sqrt(16): = 4
  • 2^10: = 1024
  • 5!: = 120

Degrees vs Radians

  • Degrees: A full circle = 360°. Common in everyday use.
  • Radians: A full circle = 2π radians. Used in higher mathematics and physics.
  • Conversion: 180° = π radians ≈ 3.14159 radians

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the calculator use degrees or radians?

You can switch between degrees and radians using the radio buttons. Degrees is the default.

How do I calculate sin, cos, and tan?

Click the sin, cos, or tan button, then enter the angle value, then close the parenthesis.

What is a factorial?

A factorial (n!) is the product of all positive integers up to n. For example, 5! = 120.
