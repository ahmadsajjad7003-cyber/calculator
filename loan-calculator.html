What is a Loan?

A loan is a sum of money borrowed from a lender (bank, credit union, or individual) that is paid back with interest over a set period. The loan amount is called the principal. The cost of borrowing is the interest. The agreed time to repay is the term.

Loans come in many forms — personal loans, student loans, auto loans, mortgages, and business loans. The formulas and calculations used here apply to any amortized loan.

Loan Payment Formula

Monthly Payment:

M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]

Where:

  • M = monthly payment
  • P = principal (loan amount)
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = total number of payments (years × 12)

Worked Example

Loan: $20,000 at 7.5% for 5 years

r = 7.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00625

n = 5 × 12 = 60

M = 20000 × [0.00625 × (1.00625)^60] / [(1.00625)^60 − 1]

M ≈ $400.76 per month

Total payment = $400.76 × 60 = $24,045.60

Total interest = $24,045.60 − $20,000 = $4,045.60

Factors That Affect Your Loan

  • Credit Score: Higher scores get lower rates.
  • Loan Term: Longer terms = lower monthly payment but higher total interest.
  • Down Payment: Larger down payments reduce the loan amount and total interest.
  • Collateral: Secured loans (backed by assets) usually have lower rates.
  • Lender: Banks, credit unions, and online lenders offer different rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a monthly loan payment calculated?

Monthly payment is calculated using the amortization formula: M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is principal, r is monthly interest rate, and n is number of payments.

What is the difference between simple and amortized loans?

Simple interest loans charge interest only on the principal. Amortized loans (like mortgages and auto loans) charge interest on the remaining balance, so early payments are mostly interest.

Does a longer loan term mean lower payments?

Yes, monthly payments are lower with longer terms, but you pay much more total interest over the life of the loan.

What is amortization?

Amortization is the process of paying off a loan through scheduled payments. Each payment covers both interest and principal. Over time, the interest portion decreases and the principal portion increases.

Can I pay off my loan early?

Most loans allow early repayment, but some charge a prepayment penalty. Check your loan agreement. Paying early reduces total interest.

What is APR vs interest rate?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) includes the interest rate plus fees and other costs, giving a fuller picture of the loan's cost.

What happens if I miss a payment?

Missing payments can lead to late fees, a lower credit score, and in serious cases, default and legal action. Contact your lender immediately if you can't make a payment.

How much can I borrow?

The amount you can borrow depends on your income, credit score, existing debts, and the lender's policies. Lenders use a debt-to-income ratio to decide.

Should I choose a fixed or variable rate?

Fixed rates stay the same for the loan's life, giving predictable payments. Variable rates can go up or down, so payments may change. Fixed is safer for most borrowers.
