What is Interest?

Interest is the cost of borrowing money, or the reward for saving money. When you deposit money in a savings account, the bank pays you interest. When you take a loan, you pay interest to the lender. There are two main types: simple interest and compound interest.

How to Use This Interest Calculator

  2. Enter the annual interest rate — as a percentage.
  3. Enter the time period — in years.
  4. Select interest type — simple or compound.
  5. Click "Calculate Interest" to see your results.

Interest Formulas

Simple Interest: A = P × (1 + rt)

Compound Interest: A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:

  • P = Principal amount
  • r = Annual interest rate (as decimal)
  • t = Time in years
  • n = Compounding frequency per year (12 for monthly)

Worked Examples

Simple Interest: $10,000 at 6% for 5 years

Interest = 10,000 × 0.06 × 5 = $3,000 (Total: $13,000)

Compound Interest (Monthly): $10,000 at 6% for 5 years

A = 10,000 × (1 + 0.06/12)^(12×5) = $13,488.50 (Interest: $3,488.50)

Notice: Compound interest earned $488.50 more than simple interest!

Simple vs. Compound Interest

  • Simple Interest: Calculated only on the principal. Grows linearly.
  • Compound Interest: Calculated on principal + accumulated interest. Grows exponentially.
  • For Savers: Compound interest is better — your money grows faster.
  • For Borrowers: Compound interest is worse — you pay more over time.

The Rule of 72

The Rule of 72 is a quick way to estimate how long it will take for an investment to double. Simply divide 72 by the annual interest rate:

  • At 6% interest: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years to double
  • At 8% interest: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years to double
  • At 12% interest: 72 ÷ 12 = 6 years to double

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal. Compound interest is calculated on principal plus accumulated interest, so it grows faster.

How is compound interest calculated?

Compound interest uses A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding frequency, and t is time.

What is the rule of 72?

Divide 72 by the interest rate to estimate how many years it takes for your money to double.

Which is better: simple or compound interest?

For savers, compound interest is better. For borrowers, compound interest costs more.
