What is BMI?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It is a simple numerical value calculated from a person's weight and height. BMI is widely used by healthcare professionals around the world as a quick screening tool to categorize adults into weight ranges: underweight, normal weight, overweight, and obese. While BMI does not directly measure body fat, it correlates strongly with more direct measures of body fatness and is an inexpensive and easy-to-perform method of screening for weight categories that may lead to health problems.

Developed in the early 19th century by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet, BMI has become the standard for population-level weight assessment. The World Health Organization (WHO) uses BMI as the primary metric for obesity classification worldwide.

BMI Formula

Metric Formula: BMI = weight (kg) / [height (m)]²

Imperial Formula: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) / [height (in)]²

Worked Example

Let's say a person weighs 70 kg and is 175 cm tall.

  1. Convert height to meters: 175 cm ÷ 100 = 1.75 m
  2. Square the height: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625 m²
  3. Divide weight by height²: 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.86

So this person's BMI is 22.86, which falls in the normal weight category.

Health Implications of BMI

Underweight (BMI below 18.5)

Being underweight can indicate malnutrition, eating disorders, or underlying health conditions. It is associated with weakened immune system, osteoporosis, anemia, and fertility issues.

Normal Weight (BMI 18.5 – 24.9)

This range is associated with the lowest risk of chronic disease. Maintaining a BMI in this range through a balanced diet and regular exercise is recommended.

Overweight (BMI 25 – 29.9)

Being overweight increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, and certain cancers. Lifestyle changes such as diet and exercise can help reduce this risk.

Obese (BMI 30 and above)

Obesity significantly increases the risk of numerous health conditions including cardiovascular disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, osteoarthritis, and certain cancers. Medical intervention is often recommended.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a general screening tool and may not be accurate for athletes (who have high muscle mass), pregnant women, elderly people, or children. For personalized health advice, consult a doctor.

What is a healthy BMI range?

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy for most adults. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25-29.9 is overweight, and 30+ is obese.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)². In imperial units: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) / height (in)².

What is BMI Prime?

BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25 (the upper limit of normal BMI). A value below 1.0 indicates a healthy weight.

Can BMI tell me my body fat percentage?

No, BMI does not measure body fat directly. For a more accurate body fat estimate, use our Body Fat Calculator.
