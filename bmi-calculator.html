What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple numerical value calculated from a person's weight and height. It was invented in the 1830s by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet and is used worldwide as a screening tool to categorize individuals as underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese.

BMI is not a diagnostic tool. It is a screening method that gives a rough indication of whether a person's weight is appropriate for their height. A doctor or healthcare provider should always be consulted for a complete health assessment.

BMI Formula

Metric System:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

Imperial System:

BMI = (Weight (lb) × 703) ÷ Height (in)²

Worked Example

Example: A person weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall.

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.86

This falls in the "Normal weight" category (18.5 – 24.9).

Limitations of BMI

  • Doesn't distinguish muscle from fat: Muscular athletes may have a high BMI despite being very fit.
  • Doesn't account for age: Older adults tend to have more body fat at the same BMI as younger adults.
  • Doesn't account for gender: Women naturally have more body fat than men at the same BMI.
  • Doesn't show fat distribution: Visceral fat (around organs) is more dangerous than subcutaneous fat, but BMI doesn't measure this.
  • Not accurate for children: Children need age- and sex-specific BMI percentile charts.
  • Ethnic differences: Health risks can appear at lower BMI values in some ethnic groups.

Why BMI Is Still Used

Despite its limitations, BMI remains a widely used screening tool because it is quick, free, and requires only weight and height. It is not meant to replace a full medical evaluation but serves as a starting point for conversations about health.

For a more complete picture, doctors may also measure waist circumference, body fat percentage, blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy BMI?

A healthy BMI is between 18.5 and 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes may be classified as overweight. It also doesn't account for age, gender, or body composition.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by your height in meters squared (kg/m²). In imperial units, it's weight in pounds multiplied by 703, divided by height in inches squared.

Can BMI be wrong?

Yes. BMI can misclassify muscular people as overweight and may miss excess fat in people with normal BMI but low muscle mass.

What is a good BMI for a woman?

The same categories apply to both men and women: 18.5–24.9 is considered healthy. However, women naturally carry more body fat than men at the same BMI.

What is a good BMI for a man?

18.5–24.9 is the healthy range for men. Men typically have more muscle mass, so some athletes may have a BMI above 25 while still being healthy.

Does BMI change with age?

BMI categories remain the same for adults, but body composition changes with age. Older adults may have more fat at the same BMI compared to younger adults.

Should I use BMI to track my fitness?

BMI is not a good measure of fitness. For fitness tracking, use body fat percentage, waist circumference, and performance metrics instead.

What should I do if my BMI is high?

Consult a doctor or dietitian. They can assess your overall health and recommend safe, sustainable changes to diet and activity.
